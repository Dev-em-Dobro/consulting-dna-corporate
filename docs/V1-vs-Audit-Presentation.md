# Corporate DNA
## Homepage **V1** — Built Against the Audit

**What was recommended vs. what was delivered**

Reference audit: *Corporate DNA website assessment* — reviewed 11 July 2026
Build reviewed: `/v1` (the bold / red homepage direction)

---

## Executive summary

The audit's core thesis: *"Corporate DNA is a much stronger firm than the current website makes it appear."* The gap is **positioning, hierarchy, editing, consistency and proof placement — not technology.**

**V1 is a direct execution of that thesis.**

- Hero, navigation, section sequence and contact form follow the audit **almost verbatim**.
- The firm is repositioned as a **leadership advisory & executive coaching** firm — not a catalogue of training programmes.
- **Proof is placed within the first two screens**, exactly as recommended.
- Facts are **consistent** with the official deck (18 / 36 / 75 / 70+ / 1,000+ / 90%).

**Two deliberate divergences** (detailed later): more motion than the audit's "restraint" guidance, and placeholder people/photography.

## The recurring benchmark formula (audit)

The best leadership-advisory sites share one trait — **decisiveness**:

> **Clear category → important buyer problem → distinctive method → credible people → measurable proof → obvious next step**

The audit's conclusion: *"CDNA possesses all six ingredients. They are simply not yet organised in that order."*

**V1's job was to put them in that order.** ✅

## Repositioning — from "training menu" to "advisory firm"

**Audit (problem):** the old homepage led with **seven equal-weight offers** (Leadership Development, Executive Coaching, Culture, High-Performing Teams, Women in Leadership, I&D, Asian Talent), presenting CDNA as a *supplier of many learning services.*

**Audit (fix):** *"Lead with what is at stake for the organisation. Then show how CDNA helps solve it."*

**V1 delivery:**
- Hero eyebrow: **"Global leadership advisory & executive coaching."**
- Four buyer-problem pathways instead of a service menu.
- Specialist offers (Women in Leadership, manager development, regional talent) are **absorbed into the enterprise story**, not given equal top-level prominence.

## Facts & editorial discipline

**Audit (problem):** conflicting figures across pages — 60 / 62 / 65 / 75 faculty, 25 vs 36 countries, 15 vs 18 years, plus grammar errors ("delivers real and results", "the the").

**V1 delivery:**
- **Single canonical set** from the deck used everywhere: **18 years · 36 countries · 75 faculty · 70+ · 1,000+ · 90%**.
- Clean, deliberate copy; **consistent British English** ("organisation", "programme", "candour").
- No duplicated headings or broken phrases.

> *"For a premium consultancy, every sentence should feel deliberate."* — audit

---

## Deliberate divergences (1/2) — Motion

**Audit position:** *"Keep animations very subtle or eliminate them entirely"* (417); *"does not need expensive animation… needs clarity, confidence, restraint"* (78); "complex animation" listed under **Defer**.

**V1 reality:** scroll-reveal section entrances, a hero intro timeline, an infinite client marquee, and count-up statistics — **added on request after the initial build.**

**Mitigations already in place:**
- All motion respects **`prefers-reduced-motion`** (fully disabled for those users).
- Entrances are transform/opacity only (no layout shift, no CLS).

**Decision point:** keep the current motion, or ship a **"restraint" variant** closer to the audit's guidance.

---

## Deliberate divergences (2/2) — People & photography

**Audit:** feature the **5–6 named senior people** a CEO/CHRO would actually meet; use **authentic leadership photography**, fewer and larger; use a **named** senior testimonial (the deck has approved ones).

**V1 reality:**
- People section uses **placeholders** ("Leadership Advisor" + "Portrait").
- Client logos are **text** (safe: audit says use only approved logos).
- Testimonial is **anonymised** ("Group HR Director · Global consumer-goods company").

**To close:** real names + headshots, approved client logos, and one named quotation.

## Out of scope for this build

**The urgent security / SEO issue** (audit, first 48 hours):

> A privacy-policy URL indexed with an Indonesian gambling title ("OLXTOTO", "slot gacor") — a possible WordPress compromise or stale hacked-content index.

- This concerns the **existing WordPress site**, not this new Next.js codebase.
- Still a **real, separate ops priority**: host-level scan, Search Console review, harden WordPress, request reindexing.
- The audit is explicit: *"Do this before spending money on cosmetic work."*

## Appendix — how V1 is built

- **Framework:** Next.js 16 (App Router) + TypeScript
- **Styling:** Tailwind CSS v4, semantic brand tokens (brand / ink / paper)
- **Type:** Poppins (single family, `next/font`)
- **Motion:** GSAP + ScrollTrigger, all gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`
- **Images:** `next/image` (static imports → no layout shift)
- **Accessibility:** responsive mobile nav, visible focus rings, contrast-checked labels, keyboard-navigable
- **Routes:** `/` (version picker) · `/v1` (this build) · `/v2` (editorial alternative)
