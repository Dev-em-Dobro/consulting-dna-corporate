Prepared by Beto & Cadu / Dev em Dobro. Record of the daily of 18 Sep 2026 — every change below is built and live. Scope: the public website and the case records in the CMS.

Everything from the daily of 18 September is built and live. This note is the record of what moved, why it looks the way it does, and the short list of what is still waiting on your side.

Two of your notes turned out to be one-line asks with long answers, and both are worth two minutes of your reading: the photograph quality, in section 6, and the case covers, in section 5. Neither is finished by us alone.

1. Services — the Partners block

You said you did not like the grey background and did not like the box, and asked to try the whole block in that darker tone instead, with the marks reading against it.

That is what it does now. The section is dark end to end, the wording is white, and the five partner marks sit directly on the background — the panel that used to hold them is gone, so there is no box at all. The logos are white files, which is why the dark background is what makes them readable in the first place.

If it still does not sit right, your own fallback from the call stands: send colour versions of the five marks and the block can go light again.

2. Service pages — the closing band and the evidence

The closing band is smaller. You asked whether it could be reduced; it came down twice, and it is now a little over a third of its old height. The wording and the button are unchanged.

The band is shared with four other pages. It was made shorter only on the service pages, so Clients & Impact, Team and the case pages keep the original proportion.

The evidence block is now dark with white text, which is the "bolder" you described. It had been dark until 16 September, so this is a return rather than an experiment: the red figures and the white wording are the pairing that block was designed around.

3. Team

  • The six leadership portraits are the same height. They were not before, and the cause was not the photographs — the frames were stretching to match whichever quote in the row ran longest. The frame is now fixed, so all six match, and it was then made slightly taller.

  • The role and the region sit together on each card. There was a gap between them — "CEO Americas", then space, then "Americas" — which made one person read as three separate lines.

4. Clients & Impact

  • "27 clients across industries" is gone, as asked.
  • The case studies read as a list again. Each case sits on its own band, alternating white and soft grey, which is the "so people can tell these are different ones" you were after. The client logos are about 40% larger.

  • One error corrected along the way. The footprint beside the map was publishing "36 Countries" while the figure itself had been changed to regions on 17 September. It now reads five regions.

5. Case pages — the first covers

The nine published cases had never had a cover photograph. The hero was a solid dark panel on every one of them. The images you renamed in the Drive have now been attached, so four cases carry a photograph for the first time: GSK, Ma'aden, and both Frasers cases.

Two things follow from that, and both need you.

The other four images have nowhere to go yet. You sent adidas twice and Heineken once. There is no published case for either: adidas is not marked as reviewed in the spreadsheet, and Heineken was unpublished on 17 September along with the five other legacy cases. If you want those two back, say so and they can be written and published.

Putting the covers in exposed a fault we could not see before. On the case page, the photograph beside "What changed" was being downloaded at 300 pixels wide and then stretched to three times that height. It looked soft and small. It has been fixed, and the column it sits in is wider. The fault had existed since 16 September and had been invisible, because until today no case had a photograph in that slot.

That slot wants its own photograph. It is currently showing the same image as the hero, in a different crop, because the system has one image field per case. A second picture per case — even one — would fix it properly. For Frasers there are already two unused photographs in your Drive folder that would do.

6. The photographs themselves

You raised the stairs picture on About: the quality is low, and we agreed to try improving it with AI. We did, and the result needs a word of explanation rather than a yes.

What was done. The stairs picture on About and the team picture on Team are both sharper and roughly twice the resolution. The four new case covers were given the same treatment before being uploaded.

What we deliberately did not do. The strongest AI setting produces a visibly crisper picture, but it does not enlarge a photograph so much as rebuild it — and on the Ma'aden picture it rebuilt the faces. The people in that room came out with features that are not theirs. That is not something to publish in a client's case study, so every image here uses a conservative setting: half the AI result blended with a conventional enlargement. It is a real improvement, and the faces stay the faces.

None of this invents detail that the camera never captured. The Ma'aden photograph is a wide crop at low resolution, and it remains the weakest image on the site. If the original file exists on someone's phone, it would beat anything we can do to the copy we have.

7. One warning about the CMS

The four cases that now have covers should not be opened and saved in the CMS panel until we have extended it.

The reason is that the case form in the panel knows only a handful of the fields those cases actually carry. Saving from the panel rewrites the record with what the form knows, and everything else — the cover, the challenge, the approach, the outcome, the figures — is dropped. This is not new and it is not caused by the covers; the covers have simply joined the list of things at risk. Editing any other content type is unaffected.

We will extend the form. Until then, tell us what needs changing on those four and we will do it directly.
