======================================================================
WHAT "CLARITY AND VISUAL APPEAL" MEANS IN PRACTICE - SIX CONCRETE THINGS
======================================================================

1. TWO TYPE FAMILIES, ONE OF THEM A SERIF.
   Headlines in a geometric sans; body text in a SERIF with generous line height. That
   pairing is what gives the editorial, publication feel instead of a SaaS feel.
   Corporate DNA's site uses one typeface, Poppins, for everything.

2. ONE ACCENT COLOUR, USED SPARINGLY.
   Dark petrol base, white, light grey. Coral appears on maybe 5% of the screen - the
   eyebrow, the numerals 01 02 03, a 2px rule at the top of a card. NEVER as a section
   background.

3. PHOTOGRAPHY OF THE WORK HAPPENING.
   Facilitator on stage, participants at the table, the group in the room, the boat in
   the Tasman. All their own, no stock. In the hero the photo is DARKENED HEAVILY,
   almost monochrome, so white text sits flat on top of it.

4. ONE SECTION PATTERN, REPEATED TOP TO BOTTOM.
   Eyebrow in caps -> large headline ending in a full stop -> serif subtitle, centred
   -> grid. Every section. That repetition is where the feeling of "clarity" actually
   comes from: nothing on the page has to be re-learned.

5. COPY WITH A NUMBER INSIDE IT.
   "40,000 participants across 25 countries." "20,000 leaders, 5 continents, 8 months."
   No generic consulting sentence survives without a figure next to it.

6. ORGANISED BY CLIENT MOMENT, NOT BY PRODUCT.
   Their main section is called "The moments we partner in", and the cards are:
   Aligning leaders at scale / Driving growth ambitions / Defining culture / Leading
   transformation / Sustaining behaviour change / Influencing at the highest level.
   The client recognises themselves before having to understand the catalogue.

======================================================================
WHERE THE REAL GAPS ARE - FOUR OF THEM, WITH HONEST COST
======================================================================

1. THE HERO HAS NO CONTRAST.
   The iceberg photo has a bright sky and the white text disappears into it. That is
   literally the opposite of "clarity", and it is the first second of the site anyone
   sees. Darkening the image fixes it. COST: HOURS.

2. RED IS USED AS A BACKGROUND, NOT AS AN ACCENT.
   The cases section is a solid red block, the nav bar is red, the numbers are red, the
   links are red. In the reference, the accent never becomes an area - it only marks.
   Reducing red to marks and letting white breathe is the highest effect for the lowest
   effort. COST: DAYS.

3. THE PHOTOGRAPHY SHOWS THE PEOPLE, NOT THE WORK.
   The portraits are posed and the team photo is a dinner. No image shows a session
   happening - room, flip chart, facilitator standing, group working. THIS IS NOT CODE,
   IT IS AN ASSET LIBRARY. Either Corporate DNA has those photos somewhere, or someone
   has to photograph the next delivery. THIS IS A QUESTION FOR HER, NOT A TASK FOR US.

4. THE AWARDS SCROLL PAST IN A TICKER.
   The moving strip at the top truncates the text and cannot be read. Explore
   Performance shows the same awards as fixed badges with context. Swapping the ticker
   for a badge block is cheap credibility. COST: HOURS.

BONUS, AND IT IS A BUG RATHER THAN A DESIGN QUESTION: the cookie banner is pinned to
the bottom of the screen and covers content on every scroll. It appears in every
screenshot. Worth fixing before any aesthetic discussion.

======================================================================
THE OPEN QUESTION - AND IT DECIDES EVERYTHING
======================================================================

"Clarity" could mean the LOOK of the page, or it could mean that you UNDERSTAND WHAT
THEY DO IN TEN SECONDS. Those two readings lead to completely different work, and her
message does not distinguish between them. Finding out which one she means is worth
more than any conclusion reached without her.

======================================================================
THE RISK IN THIS CONVERSATION, AND THE LINE THAT CONTAINS IT
======================================================================

Her message ends "Nitin/Guilly/JP - please be inspired" - three people, no specific
request. That is the seed of an open-ended redesign, four weeks before a cutover with
a hard date.

The response that holds both things together:

  "Great reference, and we can bring a lot of it across. Let me split it into two
   lists: what can be done BEFORE we switch the domain without moving the date - hero
   contrast, the use of red, the award badges - and what is content work, mainly
   photography of programmes actually running, which depends on an asset library I
   don't have. Do you want me to bring both lists with timings?"

This agrees with her, delivers something immediately, and turns a diffuse request into
two lists with named owners - without ever saying no, and without opening the schedule.

WHAT SHE ASKED, IN SUBSTANCE
======================================================================

Three questions, raised over WhatsApp:

  "Can we move after 3 months or is it annual?"
  "Can we host ourselves?"
  "Can the developer host it?"

And someone on the Corporate DNA side wrote: "We should have a schematic for each part

HER CONCERN IS LEGITIMATE AND IT IS EXACTLY THE AXON SITUATION - a domain whose
administrative contact is an employee who left, access held by an outside provider, and
nobody internally holding the keys. She is right to raise it. Do not be defensive about
it; agree with her.

======================================================================
THE STRAIGHT ANSWERS
======================================================================

"CAN WE MOVE AFTER 3 MONTHS, OR IS IT ANNUAL?"
The Axon hosting is ALREADY CANCELLED, ending 30 September. Nobody has to decide to
leave; that has happened. What needs a decision is the launch date, because the ability
to roll back to the old site shrinks to zero on 30 September.

"CAN WE HOST OURSELVES?"
Yes. The new site is not WordPress. It runs on Vercel, and there is no server to
administer. The account can be in Corporate DNA's name - AND IT SHOULD BE.

"CAN THE DEVELOPER HOST IT?"
Technically yes, and today it is: three accounts are in Dev em Dobro's name. That is
precisely the dependency she is worried about, so it is better to raise it before being
asked.

======================================================================
IF THE LMS COMES UP - DO NOT AGREE TO ANYTHING YET
======================================================================

Someone suggested combining the website hosting with the LMS hosting. DO NOT AGREE TO
THAT IN THE MEETING WITHOUT KNOWING WHAT THE LMS IS. DNS was checked on 3 September and
no LMS subdomain resolves under any likely name, so either it runs on another domain or
under a name we could not guess.

Combining them only makes sense if the LMS is WordPress. If it is a ready-made platform
- Moodle, TalentLMS, Docebo, Teachable - there is no such thing as "shared hosting";
they are two separate services, and promising it now creates a problem later.

The right question is: "WHICH LMS IS IT, WHO BOUGHT IT, AND IS IT LIVE TODAY?"

And one serious case to rule out: IF THE LMS RUNS ON THE SAME AXON SERVER AS THE
WORDPRESS SITE, IT GOES DOWN WITH IT ON 30 SEPTEMBER. That has nothing to do with DNS
and would be the most serious thing in this whole project.
