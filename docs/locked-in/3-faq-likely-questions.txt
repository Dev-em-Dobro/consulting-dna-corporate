======================================================================
ABOUT LEAVING AXON AND DEPENDING ON A PROVIDER
======================================================================

Q: "Are we still tied to Axon?"
A: Only for the domain registration, and only because moving it is unnecessary risk
   right now. The hosting is already cancelled and ends 30 September. Email never
   touched Axon - it is Microsoft 365.

Q: "Why keep the domain with them at all?"
A: Because a domain transfer is a separate operation with its own risk, and it buys
   nothing today. What matters is who controls the record, and that is what we are
   fixing: the administrative contact moves from Allison Vickery, who has left, to
   Rhea. It costs nothing.

Q: "What happens if Axon stops cooperating tomorrow?"
A: The website does not depend on them at all - it is already built and running
   elsewhere. Two things would be harder: getting an archive of the old WordPress
   site, and finding out who holds the Google Search Console property. Neither would
   stop the launch.

Q: "Can we host the site ourselves?"
A: Yes. The new site is not WordPress, there is no server to administer, and the
   account can be in Corporate DNA's name. That is what we are proposing.

Q: "So the developer holds our accounts?"
A: Today, three of them - Vercel, Supabase and Resend. We are declaring that openly and
   there is a transfer plan for each: a Corporate DNA account under
   singapore@corporatednaconsulting.com, with Nitin and Nicole as administrators.
   Corporate DNA owns; partners manage. That is the rule Guilherme set on 1 September,
   and we are executing it.

Q: "How do we avoid ending up here again?"
A: One institutional mailbox owns every account, at least two internal administrators
   on each, and a written schematic of the landscape. The schematic exists - providers,
   account holders and internal owners, per layer.

======================================================================
ABOUT THE LAUNCH AND THE SCHEDULE
======================================================================

Q: "When can we go live?"
A: The technical side is ready to schedule. What is not ready is content - several
   Solutions and case-study fields are still waiting on Corporate DNA. The date follows
   the content, not the other way around.

Q: "What is the deadline?"
A: 30 September, but not for the reason people assume. The new site does not stop
   working on that date. What ends is the ability to roll back to the old site if
   something goes wrong. Launch on 16 September and there is a full two-week safety
   net; launch on 23 September and it is one week; from 30 September there is none.

Q: "Can we buy more time?"
A: Yes, cheaply. Axon will extend the hosting for one quarter - 1 October to 31
   December - for SGD 150. There is no monthly option. Corporate DNA answers by 20
   September. The recommendation is to take it: it is the cheapest item in the project
   and the only one that buys back schedule freedom.

Q: "Will the site go down during the switch?"
A: No. Two DNS records change and traffic moves to the new site. Email is not touched.

Q: "Will we lose our Google ranking?"
A: No, and this was designed for from the start. 52 redirects are mapped from every URL
   that changes, so links and rankings carry over. Titles, meta descriptions, canonical
   tags, structured data, sitemap and robots file all ship with the site.

Q: "Will we lose our analytics history?"
A: No. Axon raised this and it is not correct. Analytics data lives in the Google
   account, not on their server. Turning the hosting off deletes nothing. Nicole now has
   administrator access to that account. The one real limitation is Google's own: the
   old Universal Analytics property is discontinued and cannot be converted - that is
   true for every company in the world, not just Corporate DNA.

======================================================================
ABOUT THE REFERENCE SITE SHE SENT
======================================================================

Q: "Did you look at Explore Performance?"
A: Yes, in detail. They are strong, and the good news is the argument structure is
   already the same as Corporate DNA's - eyebrow headings, big proof numbers,
   per-client metrics, organised by client problem. The difference is visual finish,
   not architecture. Nothing has to be thrown away.

Q: "What exactly is different?"
A: Four things. Their hero photo is darkened so the text reads; ours has a bright sky
   and the white text disappears. They use one accent colour as a mark; we use red as
   whole backgrounds. Their photography shows programmes happening; ours shows posed
   portraits. They show awards as fixed badges; ours scroll past in a ticker that
   cannot be read.

Q: "Can we have what they have?"
A: Most of it, yes. Hero contrast, the use of red and the award badges can be done
   before the domain switch without moving the date. The photography is the one that is
   not a code problem - it depends on whether Corporate DNA has images of programmes
   actually running.

Q: "Do we have those photos?"
A: QUESTION TO ASK HER. This is the item that either unlocks or blocks the biggest
   visual difference, and only Corporate DNA can answer it.

Q: "Does this change the launch date?"
A: It does not have to. The visual items are hours and days, not weeks, and they can
   land before the switch. The content and photography work is separate and can
   continue after.

Q: "Should we rebuild it like theirs?"
A: No - and it would cost more than it returns. Corporate DNA's client list is
   stronger, the case metrics are stronger, and the structure is already right. What is
   missing is finish.
